Introduction to Jalgaon District

Jalgaon District, strategically positioned as the "Gateway to North Maharashtra," is a land of profound historical resonance and industrial dynamism. Historically part of the Khandesh region, it serves as a vital cultural bridge connecting Central India with the Deccan plateau.

Beyond its world-renowned UNESCO archaeological sites, the district is a tapestry of fertile black soil plains, the serene Satpura mountain ranges, and the life-giving Tapi river. Today, it stands as a testament to the coexistence of ancient spiritual heritage and modern agricultural excellence, being the largest producer of bananas in India.

The Heritage of Khandesh

The history of Jalgaon is synonymous with the ancient region of Khandesh, a name derived from "Kanha-Desh" or the Land of Krishna. From the Satavahana era, where it served as a crucial trade hub for silk and spices, through to the Vakataka and Rashtrakuta dynasties, the district has been a crucible of artistic expression.

The medieval period saw Jalgaon rise under the Faruqi kings, who built the iconic forts and structures that still dot the landscape. Later, as part of the Maratha Empire, the region developed a distinct resilient character, blending diverse cultural influences into the unique "Khandeshi" identity we see today.

How to Reach

By Air

Jalgaon Airport connects the city with major metropolitan hubs. For international travelers, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport in Mumbai serves as the primary gateway.

  • Regular Flights
  • 20 min from city
train

By Train

Jalgaon Railway Junction is a major stop on the Central Railway network, providing direct connectivity to Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, and major South Indian cities.

  • Major Junction
  • 50+ Trains daily
directions_bus

By Road

Situated on National Highway 6, Jalgaon is easily accessible by luxury buses and private cabs from Mumbai (400km), Pune (350km), and Indore (250km).

  • NH6 Connectivity
  • Interstate Bus Hub
